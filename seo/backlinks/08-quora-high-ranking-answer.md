## Answer Copy:

**The short answer:**  
There is no universal "good ROAS." A 2.0x ROAS can be wildly profitable for a luxury jewelry brand with an 85% gross margin, while a 3.5x ROAS can bankrupt a consumer electronics brand operating with a 15% margin.

A **"good" ROAS** is simply any ROAS that sits comfortably **above your business’s specific Break-Even ROAS** while leaving you with your desired net profit margin.

Here is how you calculate your exact break-even point step-by-step:

---

### Step 1: Calculate Your Contribution Margin Before Advertising

Most people mistakenly calculate ROAS as:
$$\text{Revenue} / \text{Ad Spend}$$

This ignores real operating costs. To find your true margin, sum up:
1. **COGS %**: What the raw product costs you.
2. **Shipping & Packaging %**: Courier freight, boxes, bubble wrap.
3. **Payment Gateway %**: 2% to 3% for Stripe, Razorpay, or Cashfree.
4. **Return & RTO %**: Return-to-origin rates (typically 5%–10% for prepaid orders, 20%–35% for cash-on-delivery).

$$\text{Contribution Margin} = 100\% - (\text{COGS} + \text{Shipping} + \text{Payment Fee} + \text{Returns})$$

### Step 2: Use the Inversion Formula

$$\text{Break-Even ROAS} = \frac{1}{\text{Contribution Margin (in decimals)}}$$

#### Real-World Benchmark:
- If your contribution margin before ad spend is **50%** (0.50):
  $$\text{Break-Even ROAS} = \frac{1}{0.50} = \mathbf{2.00x}$$
- If your contribution margin before ad spend is **30%** (0.30):
  $$\text{Break-Even ROAS} = \frac{1}{0.30} = \mathbf{3.33x}$$
- If your contribution margin before ad spend is **70%** (0.70):
  $$\text{Break-Even ROAS} = \frac{1}{0.70} = \mathbf{1.43x}$$
